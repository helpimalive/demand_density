A Novel Proxy of Latent Rental Housing Demand: Evidence from US Markets

Dear Mr. Larriva,

Thank you for submitting your manuscript to Regional Science and Urban Economics. I enjoyed reading about your new metric for residential demand, and I found your argument that this metric isn't limited in the same way as a fraction (absorption or occupancy) quite compelling. That said, I do not see a path to publication for this manuscript at RSUE. 
In reading the manuscript, I had two major concerns.  You frame the metric as being a test of how intensively rental units are occupied -- how many people per unit, if I understood correctly. However, it wasn't clear to me how what you measure empirically matches this goal.  I think (?) the ideal data would be to find the number of people in each rental housing unit and find the mean of this distribution by metro area. What you do is something different: you divide the number of metro area residents by the number of rental units in CoStar.  The number of metro area residents is a combination of renters and owners. The number of rental units in CoStar is likely not a complete count of rental units. Combining these two gives us something that seems less well defined, and I'm not sure how it relates to what it is you want to measure.
You can make progress on these measurement issues using Census data -- how does the number of rental units in CoStar compare to the number reported by the Census in decennial years? Use Census data in decennial years (and ACS in non-decennial years) to find homeownership rates by metro area. You can extrapolate homeownership to non-decennial years and scale your population number to be just renters (if that's what you want -- I wasn't entirely sure).
My second biggest concern in reading this paper was the reliance on the instrument.  I did not find the exclusion assumption -- that immigration impacts rent growth only through RDI.  Why would this be the case?  If you think you have a compelling argument as to why this is the only pathway, that would strengthen the paper. 
Just as a FYI, using data for the DC metro area (and I believe this to be true in other large metros as well) in a policy report, co-authors and I found a very large decrease in household size in the densest part of the metro during COVID. I'm curious if this relates to the narrative in your paper.
We appreciate you submitting your manuscript to Regional Science and Urban Economics and thank you for giving us the opportunity to consider your work. I wish you all the best with the manuscript going forward.

Kind regards,  
Leah Brooks  
Co-Editor
